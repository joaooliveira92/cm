# Research: what post-match press conferences ask, and how managers answer

Grounds ticket 03 of the [post-match press conference effort](../../.scratch/post-match-press-conference/map.md),
which writes the question and answer templates. This note records what real conferences contain. It
writes no game copy, sets no template counts, and decides nothing about the game.

## Labels

- **Transcript**: a club or governing body published the exchange in full or near-full, with each
  question shown. Liverpool FC renders questions as "On ..." headings, so the question topic is
  first-party but the wording is the club's paraphrase.
- **Verbatim Q**: the interviewer's own words survive in the source.
- **Write-up**: a club, league or wire report that quotes the manager but not every question.
- **Inferred**: my reading across several sources. No single source says it.

Most sources are English Premier League. See *Gaps* for what that leaves out.

## How a conference runs

- **Length.** The club transcripts carry five to eight questions per conference (L1 has 8, L3 has 8,
  L7 has 7, L4 and L2 have 5, S1 has 4). Transcript.
- **Opener.** The first question is nearly always open and about the whole match: "On the game", "On
  how he would sum up the game", "On his feelings after the game" (L2, L4, L5, L7). After a defeat
  the opener is often a declarative inviting the manager to say how bad it feels: "Steve you must
  feel pig sick?", "Martin that must feel like a real kick in the teeth?" (H1, verbatim Q).
- **Order after the opener.** Inferred from L1–L7: specific incidents (a goal, a scorer, a
  substitution, a card, an injury) come next, then the bigger picture (table, run of form, the
  season), and the last question often looks ahead to the next fixture or a break (L6, L7).
- **Topic control sits with the manager.** H1 finds that interviewers ask few questions and mostly
  open ones, and that "manager interviewees devote proportionally more time to not answering the
  questions than they do to answering". There is "no fighting over topics": the journalist does
  not insist on an answer.
- **The manager steers away from blame.** H1: "whenever managers feel they are about to criticise
  others (referees, opponents, their own team) they switch to a more positive line, like praising
  team performance". O'Neill, asked "What changed?", names a foul that was not given, without naming
  the referee, then moves to "a glowing tribute to his team".
- **Feelings are expressed, not suppressed.** H1 finds managers "readily express feelings of
  disappointment, injustice, frustration and optimism".
- **Who attends.** Under the Premier League handbook as reported in N6, a manager sent off is not
  made available; an assistant does the media duties. In Germany the club and league write-ups pair
  both head coaches' statements for one match (B1). Write-up.

## Questions by trigger

Each group lists what journalists asked, the sources, and how managers answered. "Register" uses
the terms in *Answer registers* below.

### 1. Result and margin

Every conference opens here. The framing changes with the result.

- **Win.** Opener about the performance; follow-ups ask what the win means for confidence or
  whether it is the kind of result that wins titles. "On what this victory does for squad
  confidence" (L5); "On whether results like this 'win titles'" (L4); "On confidence impact" (L7).
  Registers: praise of the team, then **looking ahead** ("After the press conference, I start
  thinking about Crystal Palace", L5), and **downplaying** ("Goal difference doesn't look like we won
  9-0", L7).
- **Big win.** Klopp after 7-0: "Freak result, top performance" (L7). Questions turn to individual
  attackers and to whether the manager told players to keep pushing ("On instructions to Robertson
  mid-game", L7). Register: praise, kept measured.
- **Draw.** Questions probe whether the team sat back after leading: "On the plan after the opening
  goal and whether he told the team to 'slow the game down'" (L1). Registers: **defending the
  approach**, crediting the opponent's spell of control (L1); acceptance ("I'm happy with the point",
  Kovač, B1).
- **Defeat.** Opener invites the manager to state the feeling (H1). Follow-ups ask what went wrong
  and whether frustration "is starting to become a problem" (L1). Registers range from
  **self-criticism** ("we didn't even know who to take off", L6) through **criticism of own players**
  (Ten Hag after 0-7: "11 individuals losing their heads. That is unprofessional, that is not
  Manchester United", N3) to **normalising** ("you lose games... it's just a normal thing", L6).
- **Heavy defeat.** The losing manager names a turning point and publicly holds players to a
  standard (N3). Glass, conceding three to Celtic, split it into a poor first half and an improved
  second (A1). Inferred: the sharper the margin, the more likely the manager criticises his own side
  rather than the officials.

### 2. Late or decisive goal

- Questions ask about the emotion of the moment and about the team's habit of scoring late: "On what
  makes the side keep going until the end" (L5); "How emotional was it when the winning goal did go
  in" (F1, verbatim Q, TV interview).
- Registers: **joy kept in check** ("I couldn't feel better", L5); **resisting the narrative**
  (Klopp argued late goals were not a pattern that season, L5); valuing grit over style ("These wins
  mean more to me than when we play good football", L4).
- Conceding late, the questions turn to fatigue and squad depth (L6, extra-time defeat). Register:
  explanation by circumstance (fixture congestion).

### 3. Scorer: debut, first goal, brace, milestone

- **Debut or first goal.** "On Ngumoha's winner" for a 16-year-old's first goal, the club's youngest
  ever scorer (L4); "On Isak's first Premier League goal at Anfield" (L2); "On Rhys Williams'
  performance" for a young defender drafted in (L5). Ten Hag on Zirkzee's debut winner: "It is very
  good for a striker to come into Manchester United and to score the winning goal in his first game"
  (N4).
  Registers: **praise with protection**: the manager credits the player and the team around him and
  avoids loading expectation ("It's special for him to score in a moment like this", L4).
- **Brace or more.** "On Rodrygo's performance" after two goals (S1). Ancelotti praised his value
  "beyond goals". Klopp's 7-0 conference took three two-goal scorers together ("On front three and
  midfielders", L7).
- **Defending a scorer.** "On Firmino's winning goal and his belief" (L5): the question presupposes
  doubt; Klopp rejects it ("I'm not surprised Bobby scores goals"). Register: **loyal defence of a
  player**.
- **Club records.** The record is the news hook when one falls: the club's youngest scorer (L4), a
  club-record win and Salah becoming the club's record Premier League scorer (L7). Inferred:
  milestones are asked about when the club or league has publicised the number.

### 4. Dismissal (red card)

- **Opponent sent off.** Questions ask why the team did not exploit the extra man: "On being under
  pressure despite numerical advantage", "On Tottenham going down to nine men" (L3); "On whether
  disappointed Liverpool didn't control second half" after Newcastle's red card (L4). Registers:
  **self-criticism** ("Every time when we had the ball, we kicked it away", L3), **wry humour**
  ("it looked as if we were down to nine and they with 11", L3), crediting the opponent's spirit.
- **Opponent's red card on your player.** Arteta on Davis's challenge on Saka: "I don't think it's
  intentional at all but it's dangerous" (N2). Register: measured; not intentional, still dangerous.
- **Own player sent off.** Arteta after Rice's second yellow: "the decision that changes the game
  completely" (P2). Register: **criticism of the decision**, framed as inconsistency rather than
  bias (see *Rules that shape answers*).
- **Asked about the card decisions in general.** "On the red-card decisions" (L3). Slot: "You always
  rely a little bit on who is the VAR". Register: criticism of consistency.
- **Manager sent off.** No conference: the manager is not made available (N6).

### 5. Injury

- The most consistently present factual question. Asked whenever a player went off: "On the injury
  to Isak sustained as he scored" (L3); "On Ibrahima Konate's injury and potential returns" (L1);
  "On whether Jeremie Frimpong was substituted due to injury" (L3); "On substituting Rio Ngumoha ...
  and whether he was injured" (L1).
- Registers: **cautious update** ("if a player doesn't even try to come back that's usually not a
  good thing", L3); **reassurance** ("He's a bit sore but it's nothing serious", Arteta on Saka, N2);
  **plain correction**: it was cramp, or it was tactical ("I didn't want to go down to 10 men for 30
  seconds", L3).
- Follow-ups ask whether the player has said anything yet (L3). Managers usually have not seen the
  medical staff by then and say so.

### 6. Refereeing decision

- Asked whenever a decision shaped a goal: "On Palace's goal and Woodman down" (L2); "On the
  red-card decisions" (L3). Journalists also put the other manager's view to them: "On Glasner's
  'dangerous for football' comment" (L2).
- Managers volunteer it even without a question (Glass questioned an offside and a foul before the
  third goal, A1; Klopp brought up the referee in his first answer, F1).
- Registers:
  - **Criticism of consistency or standards**: "It's become a tactic in football" (L2); "You always
    rely a little bit on who is the VAR" (L3); a decision "that changes the game completely" (P2).
  - **Deflection**: naming the incident but not the official (H1, O'Neill).
  - **Refusal**: "now I will not saying anything about it. The refs don't say what is said, so I
    don't say what is said" (F1). Klopp's last answer: "I cannot help you now, with on top, more
    information".
  - **Pressing.** In F1 the interviewer keeps returning ("Go on", "did he say something personal to
    you there?", "The point is you mentioned Paul Tierney before"). This is the one recorded case
    here where the journalist does not let the topic go.

### 7. League position and run of form

- Asked once the match is covered: "On pushing for the top four again" (L3); "On whether
  he 'has full confidence' he can get fans 'back onside'" and "On hearing boos multiple times at
  Anfield" (L1); losing runs ("the most concerning thing is that you lose four times in a row", P1).
- Relegation: Taylor at Exeter, "a long term picture that we haven't been good enough" (E1).
- Registers: **forward-looking deflection** (Slot: improvement comes next season with summer
  recruitment, L1); **realism** ("we are definitely not perfect yet", L3); **accepting
  responsibility** (E1).
- A pre-season claim gets thrown back after a loss: Postecoglou, asked about saying he usually wins
  something in his second year, answered "I don't usually win things, I always win things in my
  second year" (N1). Register: **defiance**.
- Rivalry form also triggers a question: "On whether the win resembled last season's dramatic
  victories" (L5).

### 8. Opponent's standing

- Against a stronger side, the losing or drawing manager credits the opponent's quality (Kovač on
  scoring "especially here in Munich", B1; Bruce asked "How many other teams have you seen push the
  champions that far?", H1, verbatim Q).
- An underdog win is framed as the long shot coming off: Cowley after non-league Lincoln won at
  Burnley, "We said it was a one in a 100 chance and thankfully we got that opportunity" (N5).
- Next opponents come up at the end when a draw or tie is known: "On potential knockout opponents"
  (S1); "On Real Madrid second leg" (L7, dismissed to focus on the next league game).
- Thin: no first-party transcript was found where the opponent's league position was the explicit
  subject of a question. See *Gaps*.

### Not detectable from the match record

These appear in real conferences but have no trigger in a recorded match. Listed so ticket 03 can
rule them out knowingly: crowd reaction and boos (L1), a player's ovation (S1), competition format
(S1), the manager's own future (L6), positional experiments ("On Dominik Szoboszlai playing
right-back", L4: detectable only if the game records the position played).

## Answer registers

The recurring tones across the sources above. "Where seen" lists the triggers in which each
register occurred.

| Register | What it sounds like | Where seen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Praise of the team | effort, character, "kept on fighting" | result (win, draw), late goal (L2, L4, L5, L7) |
| Praise of an individual | named player, with protection from hype | scorer, debut (L4, L5, N4, S1) |
| Credit to the opponent | the other side's quality or plan | draw, defeat, opponent standing (L1, L4, B1) |
| Self-criticism | "we kicked it away", "we were not good enough" | defeat, numerical advantage (L3, L6, E1) |
| Criticism of own players | standards, professionalism | heavy defeat (N3) |
| Criticism of officials | consistency, standards, "a tactic" | referee, red card (L2, L3, P2, A1, F2) |
| Deflection | answers a nearby question, or turns to positives | any; post-defeat especially (H1, L1) |
| Refusal | declines to say more | referee (F1) |
| Downplaying | "freak result", "not a pattern" | big win, late goal (L5, L7) |
| Looking ahead | next game, next season | close of most conferences (L1, L5, L7) |
| Humour, wry | dry observation, often self-directed | red card on opponent (L3) |
| Defiance | doubles down on a claim | form, pressure (N1, N7) |

H2 adds that managers of the biggest clubs use more assertive language after matches, and that a
manager seen as weak in these exchanges is read as off-brand for the club (abstract only).

Mourinho's walk-out after a 0-3 home defeat (N7) is the extreme case of defiance; it ends the
conference.

## Rules that shape answers

The deflection and refusal registers around referees have a regulatory cause in England. The FA's
2025/26 guidance to managers, quoted in F2, says these public comments "may lead to disciplinary
charges":

> "Implication of bias: any comment which implies or alleges bias on the part of a Match Official."
> "Questioning integrity: any comment which questions the integrity of a Match Official."
> "Personal/offensive: comments which are personally offensive, particularly in relation to Match
> Officials. This can also include repeated negative and/or inflammatory comments in respect of the
> Match Official's performance."

Charges follow under FA Rule E3.1. Silva was given a one-match touchline ban and a fine of £120,000,
reduced to £90,000, for naming the VAR and implying bias (F2). Klopp was charged for saying of the
referee "I really don't know what this man has with us" and hinting at something said to him; the
Commission accepted his apology and noted he had spoken "in the heat of the moment" (F1). So the
safe criticism is of consistency or of the decision, and the unsafe one is of the person.

## Gaps

- **arsenal.com transcripts were unreachable.** The node URLs that search engines index returned 404,
  so Arteta's answers here come from league and secondary reports (P2, N2).
- **Questions are mostly paraphrased.** Liverpool FC, the richest first-party source, gives question
  topics as "On ..." headings. Verbatim journalist wording survives only in H1 (post-match TV
  interviews) and F1 (TV interview). Both are one-to-one broadcast interviews, not the written-press
  conference, though H1 treats them as the same genre.
- **Opponent standing as an explicit question** was not found in a first-party transcript; the
  examples are a write-up (B1), a broadcast interview (N5) and H1.
- **Humour** is thin in the record. One first-party instance (L3); the Postecoglou example (N1) is
  closer to defiance and is from a secondary report.
- **Own-player red cards** have only league and secondary coverage (P2, N2), no full transcript.
- **Penalty decisions** specifically (awarded or denied) did not surface in a fetched transcript.
  They fall under *Refereeing decision* by inference.
- **Coverage is English-top-flight-heavy.** One Champions League (S1), one Bundesliga write-up (B1),
  one Scottish club (A1), one League One club (E1). Lower-league and non-English conferences may run
  shorter and with fewer journalists; nothing here measures that.
- **No source counts question frequency by trigger.** The ordering and "most consistently present"
  claims are inferred from the transcripts and write-ups listed above.
